## Fatal flaws
- No buyer. §10 says the broker, employer or employee might pay, undecided. The only named payer today is a broker on commission, and that commission rate is UNKNOWN. Sources that would back it: IRDAI commission and expense regulations, insurer broker-remuneration disclosures, a broker rate card or quote.
- No rupee price, so no margin. The 1 credit (about Rs 26, person enrich) plus 5 credits per alert (about Rs 130) is the only computed cost. Job-search and web-fetch costs are not in the RULES §2 table, so the cost per job is incomplete.
- The billable event is unclear. A fact file costs at least Rs 26 and an alert Rs 130. An employee who is not paying will not fund that, and a broker paying per alert needs a commission to cover it. A job-change alert fires only on a rare event, so revenue per alert is unproven.
- Licence: the service is "a tool used by a licensed broker". That makes the broker the real buyer, but no broker has been named or priced.
- Core data is UNVERIFIED: no recorded job-search response (our own call returned HTTP 400), no person-enrich response, and the person-watch path is unchecked. The owner's 113 of 3,362 figure is not committed to fixtures.
- Scale economics are unanswered (100x, second client). Person-level watching at 5 credits per alert grows linearly, and the idea overlaps with #14, which was dropped.
- Sprawl: employer benchmark, employee desk, claims helper and shipment cover. None of these is priced.
