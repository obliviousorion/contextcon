  The theme: Services, Replaced.
Services are at a turning point. For twenty years, software helped recruiters, analysts and diligence firms
do their jobs. Now it’s starting to do the jobs itself.

Companies spend far more on services than on software. Recruiting firms, auditors, insurance brokers and
freight forwarders are paid for an outcome, not a tool, and agents can now deliver it. The latest models
finish a full day’s work on their own, act through email, calls and payments, and look up who someone is
and what a company does in one canonical graph.

Building a service is different from building a tool: parts delivered rather than suppliers listed, invoices paid
rather than invoices read, fraud caught rather than flagged, offers signed rather than candidates sourced.

Pick a job people do today, in-house or at a firm, from hiring to underwriting to freight, and build the
agents that do all of it. Bring in a human only for the exceptions, make every decision cite its reasons, and
do it all in seven hours.


What you could build
   ●​ A recruiter that takes a role and returns an accepted offer
   ●​ A diligence team that turns a data room into the red flags a partner needs before the second
       meeting
   ●​ An underwriter that checks every submission against the world, then quotes, refers or declines
   ●​ A scout that finds exceptional founders before they raise, and says why each one deserves a
       meeting
   ●​ A fraud investigator that takes an insider tip and returns a complaint-ready case file

  Annexure B. The themes in full
Agents that turn a whole service into software. Build inside a theme, or bring your own idea: any
service people are paid to do is fair game.

1. Hiring
Recruiting firms earn their fee when a person starts the job; a stack of profiles earns nothing. The right
engineer calls the job something else, changed roles last month and shares a name with two strangers.

Build an agent that fills roles with no one at the keyboard:

   ●​ 1.1 The Search Desk: From a manager’s three lines to a first call, tested on real hires.
   ●​ 1.2 The Career Check: Every line of a CV checked against the record.

We’re looking for shortlists that hold the people companies really hired, built only from what the record
showed before each hire.



ContextCon 05: BITS Pilani. Build AI agents on live web data. Sunday 22 November 2026.                 18 of 34
2. Investing
A fund’s analysts hunt for companies early, test what founders claim and watch every company in the
portfolio. Every call rests on what was public that day, and the answer arrives years later.

Build an agent that makes a fund’s calls on its own:

   ●​ 2.1 The Pre-Round Scout: Who raises next, replayed from a past date.
   ●​ 2.2 The Diligence Desk: Every pitch-deck claim tested before the second meeting.

We’re looking for calls replayed from a past date that match what happened next, with no fact from after
that date.


3. Research and markets
Research firms and strategy teams answer questions about markets for clients who act on the answer. The
reading never ends, and a model answers from old memory with full confidence.

Build an agent that gets to the answer before the market does:

   ●​ 3.1 The Market Map: Every company in a market, once, with a cited reason.
   ●​ 3.2 The Research Desk: Dated, sourced answers, or the false premise named.

We’re looking for answers about events no model has seen, each number traced to a dated source, where
a wrong answer costs more than none.


4. Insurance
Underwriters price a business from the answers on its application, and the applicant writes those answers.
Forms understate size and leave out risky work, and checking each one by hand takes days.

Build an agent that insures a business with no one in the loop:

   ●​ 4.1 The Underwriting Desk: Quote, refer or decline, checked against the record.

We’re looking for the same decision however the applicant words the form, with a source behind every
reason.


5. Trust and compliance
Compliance analysts decide whether a business or a person is who they claim to be, before money moves.
Records share no common key, a slow source looks like a missing one, and a front company can carry
genuine papers.

Build an agent that decides who to trust:

   ●​ 5.1 The Vendor Check: A new vendor proven real, active and run by its director.
   ●​ 5.2 The Adverse Media Screen: Directors screened against the news, namesakes cleared.

We’re looking for fronts caught with evidence, while honest firms and innocent namesakes pass.

6. Buying and paying
Payables teams stand between an invoice and the company’s bank account. Duplicates slip through in
new formats, and the costliest fraud is a polite email asking to change bank details.

Build an agent you can hand the company’s books:

   ●​ 6.1 The Payables Desk: Each invoice paid once, through crashes and bank-detail fraud.

We’re looking for books an auditor can replay line by line, with no rupee paid twice or to the wrong
account.


7. Agents that spend
An agent doing real work has to pay along the way: for data, a tool, an hour of compute. Owners either
approve every purchase by hand or hand over a card with no ceiling.

Build an agent that spends on someone’s behalf with no one watching:

   ●​ 7.1 The Research Buyer: Data bought for a job inside a budget the client can cut.

We’re looking for the whole job done under a ceiling the owner watches live and can lower mid-run, with
every credit on the ledger.


8. Supply chain
Sourcing teams find manufacturers, check they are real and able to deliver, and run quotes by email and
spreadsheet. Traders pose as factories, certificates lapse, and a promise of four weeks means eight.

Build an agent that runs freight and parts operations end to end:

   ●​ 8.1 The Sourcing Desk: Real makers found, quotes run, an order recommended.

We’re looking for parts made by real factories at the quoted price and date, with every record agreeing.


9. Revenue
A sales team earns its keep by reaching the person who owns a problem in the week it turns urgent. The
person and the week both keep changing, and buyers ignore a generic message.

Build an agent that brings in revenue on its own:

   ●​ 9.1 The Outbound Desk: Meetings booked on triggers from the last 30 days.
   ●​ 9.2 The Inbound Desk: Every inbound lead answered and routed within two minutes.

We’re looking for meetings with the right person, where every claim in every message traces to a dated
source.


10. Infrastructure for agents
Each service above leans on the same few parts, and most of those parts are still missing. An agent that
cannot show where a fact came from, or check an action before it happens, cannot run a service alone.

Build the building blocks every other agent needs:

   ●​ 10.1 The Evidence Ledger: Where every fact came from, re-checked on demand.

We’re looking for parts that keep their promises through a crash, a change of model and a poisoned input.
